Fursay editorial method

How the team selects, checks, translates, updates, and corrects parent-facing material

Fursay Family Learning Editorial Team · Updated: 2026-09-25

Choose a specific family question

Each guide starts from a recognizable moment and gives adults a small option they can try without buying anything. We write for parents and caregivers rather than promising a result for a child.

Check relevant sources

We prefer official organizations, original research, and professional guidance. Each source note explains which principle it supports and what it cannot establish about a Fursay activity or an individual family.

Review three language versions

The team checks English, Traditional Chinese, and Arabic wording for clarity, local context, and usable steps. Translation does not replace professional review where a specialist judgment is needed, and we do not invent individual qualifications.

Keep boundaries visible

Before publication we review examples, limitations, affiliate disclosures, links, accessibility, and structured data. Guides are parent-facing ideas, not diagnosis, therapy, or guaranteed instruction.

Correct and update responsibly

Each guide lists a publication date, modification date, and its own revision note. Readers can report a specific sentence through Contact. A material correction updates the page, structured data, and sitemap date; a major content deployment restarts the 14-day stability period used in review preparation.
